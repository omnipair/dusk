Dusk · design experiment · 31 October 2026

Junior capital.
Configurable per market.

Research and current specification · native integration in progress

Use the V1 rate-controller mechanism with governance-adjustable parameters for each market. Omitted inputs default to a 14-day half-life, 4% initial APR, a 2% floor and no configured ceiling. The default 5–6.25% signal band expresses 20–25% junior capital at 80% reference utilization.

1,514 synthetic simulations were run, including repeated baseline configurations. These earlier experiments used different rate bounds and half-lives. They remain historical evidence and do not validate the new uncapped defaults. The current specification is documented separately; there is no universal immutable configuration to optimize.

Both band bounds, half-life, initial APR, minimum APR and maximum APR are configurable per market. Omitted creation inputs use these defaults; valid supplied values override them. Governance changes apply prospectively and do not reset an existing market to its initial APR. Rate adjustment starts immediately outside the band. The 5–6.25% band stays fixed until governance changes it; it is not recalculated from live utilization. The 0.05% queue fee is transferred to the permanent junior seed. The source review also records precision and update-ordering fixes needed when porting V1.

The 2% target supports more borrowing but allows a thinner junior buffer. The 7-day response can reduce losses in some stress timings; the 14-day response changes direction less often in the initial ten scenarios. Raising the target to 10% increases costs and reduces borrowing without removing early-bootstrap exposure.

Do not read the initial zero-loss cases as a guarantee. With the same 5% / 14-day parameters, the credit-stress case passes $28,612 to senior when junior providers respond to collected fees, versus $0 when they respond to projected revenue. The behavioral assumption matters.

Across the initial ten scenarios, adding coverage reduces lending and raises borrowing costs compared with the utilization-only controller, while reducing senior losses in the tested crash. That is a trade-off, not free capital efficiency. Whether users prefer it depends on the price of protection and the availability of junior capital.

All three use the same capital budget, revenue split, seed and adaptive-rate shape. Targets are 80% utilization, 25% coverage, or the combined 5% signal; these align only at 80% utilization. This isolates candidate signal behavior and is not a benchmark against Dusk’s current Morpho-like rate implementation. Only the four combined-controller profiles above received the broader behavioral sensitivity sweep.

The seed is productive, but small

With no user joining junior, the seed grows from $1,000 to $6,229 over 90 simulated days. Average borrowing falls to $247,615; peak borrow APR reaches 33.62%. The seed resolves who owns early junior fees. It does not supply the underwriting depth of a mature junior tranche.

Show the deposit’s rate, not just the tiny seed’s rate

Suppose annual net LP revenue is $40,000. Junior receives $20,000. A $1,000 seed alone shows a 2,000% annualized rate. After a user converts $100,000 into junior, the same revenue supports about 19.8% per dollar of junior capital. Neither number is a guaranteed APY.

Display a prospective rate for the user’s selected amount. Label accrued estimates separately from collected income. Zero borrowing and zero swap revenue means zero yield; do not create a positive APY from accounting alone. Seed compounding and future deposits change the rate over time.

A fee must have a real recipient

At 5 bp, a $10,000 request costs $5 if those shares move to the permanent seed. Returning the fee to remaining junior holders partially refunds the requester:

Repeatedly renewing an entire position every 9–12 days would cost roughly 1.5–2.0% of its value per year at 5 bp, before compounding or price changes. That is meaningful friction, not proof that strategic exits are unprofitable. The macro model does not optimize adversarial queue timing.

What the accounting prototype establishes

Eleven additional V1-controller reference tests pass, covering configurable bounds, optional creation inputs, uncapped rates, longer half-lives and prospective governance changes. The earlier thirty-one tests cover exact two-token balances, one shared junior budget, burning srLP on junior entry, historical income after transfers, partial collections, unpaid-interest writeoffs, non-redeemable seed claims, rebuilding junior after exhaustion, and preserving unused claim-window time during restrictions.

Income preparation and claiming are capped at eight records per call. Historical share checkpoints preserve the original owner’s entitlement. This bounds work per call; it does not eliminate history storage, catch-up transactions, or the need to verify data access and compute cost in Solana.

Still required before native integration

  • A reliable insolvency index and a rule that cannot be held indefinitely by repeated tiny distressed positions. The prototype tests the clock, not that detection/griefing solution.
  • Native handling of long-idle loan income history, integer rounding, token-generation rollover after junior exhaustion, and hLP integration. Empty-senior routing remains a separate unresolved rule.
  • Native state-transition, SDK/interface and full CI checks. No Rust program or deployed configuration has been changed for this experiment.
