Dusk hLP idle market and oracle recovery experiment
20 October 2026

RESULT

Removing arbitrage trades does not by itself break an already matched hLP
hedge. Changing the price tag changes neither asset quantities nor debt
quantities. Funding accrual does create a mismatch. A fresh reference alone
does not pay it.

There is a second, separate idle effect in the current native code. With
the 200% initial rate-at-target fixture, a market touched only at the end of
90 days develops a 14.284% funding gap, versus 3.084% with
hourly accounting updates. The one-step case crosses the 12.5% native flash
recovery threshold; the hourly case does not. These are the SAME starting
balances and native IRM, with different update schedules.

PR #52 already provides a useful independent route: a caller can fund new
public-loan income and execute the native hLP net-income settlement without
a swap. In the historical October stress, daily calls reduced the sampled
maximum base-hLP mismatch from 1.669% to
0.288% of contemporaneous target-asset HODL value.
This is still a transaction someone must submit; an oracle account updating
does not automatically execute Dusk.

Fresh recovery prices lowered the external value paid to buyers. Under the
weak-buyer October case that value fell from $755.14
to $104.48. That amount includes the buyer's
execution costs and profit, and excludes separately booked funding fees.
This is a controlled model comparison using real prices/books, not an estimate
of live bidder demand or a promise of those savings.

and supporting documentation/simulation files; there is no runtime source
difference. The runtime snapshot therefore uses PR52's head.

Both arms use that exact Rust hLP state machine: integer accrual, live reserve
claims, native adaptive IRM, new-income settlement and nonterminal flash
recovery. The small benchmark shim calls native transition functions and
settles their funding-interest receipts. It does not replace their arithmetic.
No changes were made to the working checkout's program or SDK source.

The oracle arm ONLY substitutes the external market mid for the symmetric
EMA used to price a recovery payment. The current native recovery eligibility,
curve valuation, hLP solver and discount policy remain intact.

Neither PR implements OP-GAMM. This is an idle-accounting and recovery-reference
ablation for the proposed OP-GAMM, not a completed OP-GAMM implementation or
swap benchmark. No blend coefficient is tested. Native and OP pricing policies
that only change quotes have identical balances during genuine zero-trade
intervals. Their real difference after trading resumes is outside this test.
In particular, reduced LVR does not establish lower arbitrage volume.
Neither arm earns customer or arbitrage swap fees in this experiment.
It therefore does not quantify lost trading fees, prevented LVR or the net
profitability of OP-GAMM after trading resumes.

DATA AND ASSUMPTIONS

Three disjoint public Bybit SOL/USDT spot episodes:
19 June 00:01 to 26 June 00:00 UTC 2025;
7 October 00:01 to 14 October 00:00 UTC 2025;
18 November 00:01 to 25 November 00:00 UTC 2025.
The cached source has 30,240 one-minute reconstructed top-200 books.
This replay selects hourly marks/books plus each episode's final sample:
507 real observations. No book is carried across the gaps between episodes.
Source URLs/checksums and reconstruction checks remain in
../incremental-liquidation-loss-2026-10-12/data/source-notes.md and
../incremental-liquidation-loss-2026-10-12/data/download-manifest.json.
Input hashes and exact source pins are in provenance.json.

The 90-day paths are SYNTHETIC: +50% trend, -60% trend, and a +/-50% sine
roundtrip. They use hourly native accrual, a declared 10bp execution cost
and $100,000 finite execution capacity, not fabricated historical depth.
Only the 21 historical days support conclusions about observed liquidity.
The main matrix performs hourly market accounting updates even though it
has no swaps. A separate 12-case cadence ablation compares hourly updates
with a single first update after 7, 30 or 90 untouched days, at both initial
rate settings. See cadence.csv and cadence-validation.json. Its constant
valuation mark isolates funding; arbitrary external price changes would
still not change the underlying token quantities before an actual action.

Each market starts with $1m ordinary two-sided liquidity and $20k of each
hLP target asset. The units are 9 base decimals and 6 quote decimals.
The main fixture has 4x peak concentration; 1x and 10x are also run for the
October recovery stress. There are no customer swaps, LP flows, borrower
repayments/defaults, or controller recentering. The ordinary LP tranche
remains present. Transfer fees are zero.

Initial rate-at-target is 4% or 200% APR; these are NOT constant charged
funding rates. The native utilization-sensitive IRM evolves thereafter.
The low-utilization idle cases adapt downward. Public-income cases seed
performing public loans equal to 70% of each side's available cash; this
is a balance-sheet fixture, not a borrower admission or health simulation.
Public loan claims are valued at full face value throughout.

Separate recovery stress cases inherit 13% unpaid hLP funding at inception
and have no public loans. This deliberately starts above the native 9/8
debt-to-opposite-claim threshold. It is NOT funding generated by seven days
at 4%. All reported returns in those cases include that inherited shortfall;
the large negative returns must not be attributed entirely to the replay week.

There are no buyers for the first 72 hours. Afterwards:
competitive capacity = $50,000, one opportunity every 6 hours;
weak capacity = $2,000, one opportunity daily;
absent = no recovery trade.
Candidates pay the complete native quote, use the observed finite bid/ask
book with 5bp exchange fee, and require positive profit after an assumed $2
transaction cost. Both directions are checked; the more profitable eligible
direction fills. A native session can rehedge BOTH hLP cohorts. Whole-session
capital limits are modeled assumptions; flash financing can alter them.
Terminal recovery quotes are recorded as unfilled, not invented settlements.
No terminal quotes or replay errors occurred in the final matrix.

Daily income maintenance charges a modeled $2 per call to an external
sponsor, reported separately. It is not automatically debited from hLP.
Oracle publication costs, execution failures, inclusion latency, basis risk,
adversarial ordering, oracle faults and tokenized-asset redemption constraints
are not modeled.

MEASURES

For each hLP, opposite-asset gap = indexed debt - pro-rata live opposite claim.
The gap is marked at the external price, then divided by the current value
of holding the initially deposited target asset. Negative values mean excess
opposite inventory; reported maximum drift is the maximum absolute value.
This is quantity mismatch, not a complete option-style portfolio delta.

Return = (live base claim * external price + live quote claim - marked debt
+ separately funded hLP yield) / target-asset HODL - 1.
It includes booked funding, native fees and collateral paid in recoveries.
It is a marked excess return, NOT absolute USD return or cash withdrawal P&L.
Public loan principal and unpaid income are at face value. Future fees on
unfunded income and future exit costs are not deducted. Funded yield is not
added to live reserves a second time. Historical funded claims are preserved.

Recovery cost = external-mark value of collateral delivered minus value of
repayment received. Buyer profit deducts actual modeled book execution costs
and $2 transaction cost. Funding paid, protocol income, and external transfer
are separate columns in results.csv; do not add funding paid to a return
that has already deducted the debt. Ordinary LPs receive funding income,
so funding is not entirely an external system loss.

WHAT THE RESULTS CHANGE

The clean no-funding control preserves hedge quantities to token rounding,
even during moving external prices. Both pricing arms are identical without
a recovery event. Funding, not passage of external prices alone, drives
the mismatches in the clean idle cases.

Income maintenance works without customer volume, but is a cash-funded
advance against outstanding public receivables. In the high-utilization
90-day stress, only 31 of 90 daily calls funded
new income; the last was day 31. Executable
cash fell to 0.132% of its initial post-borrow
level. Later zero-funding calls did not restore the hedge.

The very large positive marked returns in that 90-day income case depend
on compounded, fully performing unpaid public loans. They are NOT evidence
of sustainable yield or liquid profitability. The useful finding there is
cash exhaustion and renewed hedge drift.

With weak buyers, June and November recovery stress did not fill in either
pricing arm. In October, falling SOL reduced the USD financing requirement
enough for a day-five quote-target recovery. A correct price does not provide
buyer capital, market depth or a caller incentive.

The hybrid oracle-reference arm is not uniformly better for every hLP.
At the competitive October fill, the oracle reduces the external payout,
but changes which side the buyer selects. The remaining native valuation
still performs internal hedge conversions at the old curve mark. Base-hLP
returns can worsen while quote-hLP or ordinary LPs benefit.
This demonstrates why changing only the recovery reference is insufficient:
an OP-GAMM needs consistent valuation and explicit accounting for the asset
conversion across hLP and ordinary LPs. Lower external payout alone is not
proof of a fair allocation across LP cohorts.

IMPLEMENTATION DIRECTION

Keep in-swap hLP rehedging. Add no reliance on arbitrage volume for hLP
maintenance. Reuse the new-income and external recovery paths as separate
permissionless actions with an explicit caller-incentive policy.
Also specify the intended accrual cadence. Native accrue_side applies the
previously stored instantaneous rate to elapsed time before adapting the
rate-at-target (transitions/lending/mod.rs, lines 1150-1195). The 90-day result
is therefore not an oracle effect and not a bug classification; it is a
material scheduling dependency to account for in an idle-market design.
An external oracle updating its own account does not update Dusk's clock.

For OP-GAMM, next implement and test one coherent rule for oracle-relative
curve pricing, hLP valuation and recovery settlement. Include no-swap gaps,
income exhaustion, ordinary LP value transfers and resumed trading in that
test. Fresh prices improve the information used to pay for a hedge; they do
not execute or finance the hedge. This experiment is evidence for that
design direction, not approval to deploy the recovery-reference hybrid.
