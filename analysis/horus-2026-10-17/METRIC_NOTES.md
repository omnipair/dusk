# Metric OMM: implications for Horus and Dusk

Read 17 October 2026 from Metric's [current documentation](https://docs.metric.xyz/). This is a mechanism comparison, not a security audit or a Dusk implementation decision. Dusk integration remains held until PR #45 merges.

## What Metric actually does

Metric separates a pool's [Price Provider](https://docs.metric.xyz/how-pools-work/price-providers), which supplies reference, bid and ask, from [liquidity bins](https://docs.metric.xyz/how-pools-work/liquidity-and-bins), which hold assets over percentage-deviation intervals around that reference. Trades move the deviation and consume bins. A reference update changes the absolute quote while leaving the active bin, deviation, token balances and LP ownership unchanged. At +1.5% deviation, a reference move from $100 to $105 moves the pre-spread quote from $101.50 to $106.575 without a trade.

That removes the **reserve-price catch-up trade caused solely by an accurate reference move**. It does not establish zero arbitrage or zero LP loss: the existing deviation and spread can still cross an external executable market, a late/wrong reference can be picked off, and LP inventory remains exposed to future price moves. Metric's own [freshness and ordering](https://docs.metric.xyz/how-pools-work/freshness-and-ordering), [risks](https://docs.metric.xyz/use-metric/provide-and-manage-liquidity/risks-and-safeguards), and [returns](https://docs.metric.xyz/use-metric/provide-and-manage-liquidity/understanding-your-returns) pages explicitly describe these limits. Ignoring fees, a riskless cross-venue trade is possible if pool ask is below external executable bid, or pool bid is above external executable ask; moving the reference alone does not prove these inequalities are false.

Metric's [shared oracle layer](https://docs.metric.xyz/how-pools-work/oracles) exposes price, spread and timestamp to provider contracts. It supports shared Chainlink/Pyth feeds, a publisher-controlled compressed feed and a tokenized-stock multiplier adapter. The same verified update can serve multiple pools. Its Chainlink/Pyth on-chain read access is limited to registered pools and approved integrators, whereas its compressed feeds are public. This access model is Metric's commercial/operational choice, not a prerequisite for Dusk or Horus.

Metric's [anchored standard](https://docs.metric.xyz/how-pools-work/anchored-pricing) constrains a custom bid/ask against an independent feed and adds a per-bin StopLoss that rejects trades breaching a configured drawdown. Metric states that the StopLoss does not cap cumulative losses; its [risk page](https://docs.metric.xyz/use-metric/provide-and-manage-liquidity/risks-and-safeguards) notes the first monitored trade establishes a baseline and that a decaying peak permits additional losses over time. A velocity guard can reject large jumps but permits drift through smaller steps.

On participating Ethereum/BSC builder paths, Metric forwards a maker update for inclusion before trades. Its [ordering page](https://docs.metric.xyz/how-pools-work/freshness-and-ordering) identifies these as builder service commitments, not on-chain guarantees, and notes that latency and unprotected paths remain. This does not directly solve Solana ordering for Dusk. A transaction-local update-and-consume or consumer CPI is the Dusk design target, subject to SVM feasibility; otherwise freshness and exposure rules must explicitly price the residual pick-off risk.

## What to take into Dusk research

1. **Separate reference, execution quote and internal impact.** A generic provider observation may supply external price and quality; the OP-GAMM market computes its own inventory/impact response and fee. Test whether reference changes alone cause a reserve catch-up trade. Do not assume Metric's bin curve is the OP-CFMM from the paper.
2. **Keep provider neutrality.** Metric's provider abstraction supports oracle and non-oracle strategies; Dusk should pin a chosen adapter/feed per permissionless market and accept Horus only as one implementation. Curated UX labels are separate from on-chain market creation.
3. **Model the actual token claim.** For tokenized stocks, an underlying share feed may need a token-to-share multiplier that changes on splits/dividends; session pauses must stop price-led swaps without silently breaking repayment or other risk-reducing actions. Verify mint, issuer terms and feed binding.
4. **Measure both price and executable depth.** Metric's bins represent liquidity *inside its pool*. They are not proof of external liquidation capacity for Dusk leverage or loans. Horus should keep external route-level sell proceeds and buy costs as a distinct observation.
5. **Stress sequences, not only one update.** Compare LP value versus holding, execution quality, available leverage, realized credit loss and hLP equity under stale updates, incorrect reference, correlated source failure, update crossing, repeated round trips, market close/reopen and protection recovery. A stop-loss or inventory `q` can reset while cash losses accumulate.

The [OP-AMM paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.33799v1) supplies axioms for oracle response and fixed-oracle trading curves; Metric is an instructive commercial design, not evidence that any particular Dusk blend or parameter has been validated.
